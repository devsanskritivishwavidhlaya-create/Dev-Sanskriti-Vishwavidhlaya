AICTE Status & Norms

Statutory legal standing, Supreme Court mandate, and technical education compliance framework

Statutory Position Under Law

1. Legal Standing of Universities Under AICTE Act

As per the settled law of India, statutory Universities established under an Act of the State Legislature or Parliament do not require prior approval from the All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE) to commence or conduct courses in technical education, engineering, technology, computer applications, or management.

Standing of Law Applicable to Dev Sanskriti Vishwavidyalaya

Only institutions which are affiliated to Universities require AICTE approval. As Dev Sanskriti Vishwavidyalaya is an autonomous University established under a State Legislative Act and duly recognized by the University Grants Commission (UGC) under Section 2(f) of the UGC Act, 1956, it does not require prior AICTE approval to offer technical, management, and degree programs.

This legal standing has been authoritatively affirmed by the Hon’ble Supreme Court of India in the landmark judgment of Bharathidasan University & Anr. Vs All India Council for Technical Education & Ors.

Statutory Autonomy

Universities are autonomous degree-granting bodies constituted under legislative Acts and UGC regulations.

Affiliated vs. University

AICTE prior approval is mandated exclusively for affiliated institutions/colleges, not for statutory Universities.

Conformance to Norms

While prior approval is not required, DSVV diligently adheres to the standards, syllabi, and norms laid down by AICTE.

Apex Court Ruling

2. Supreme Court Landmark Judgment (Bharathidasan Case)

The statutory relationship between Universities and the AICTE was conclusively settled by the Hon’ble Supreme Court of India:

Case Reference: Appeal (Civil) 2056 of 1999

Parties: Bharathidasan University & Anr. Versus All India Council for Technical Education & Ors.

Date of Judgment: 24 September 2001

Bench: Hon'ble Mr. Justice S. Rajendra Babu & Hon'ble Mr. Justice Doraiswamy Raju

"The AICTE Act maintains a complete dichotomy between a 'University' and a 'technical institution'. When by definition a 'University' is excluded from a 'technical institution', the power under Section 10(1)(k) to grant approval for starting new technical institutions and for introduction of new courses would not cover a 'University' but only a 'technical institution'."

Key Principles Declared by the Supreme Court:

  • Exclusion of Universities from Section 2(h): The Supreme Court observed that the definition of a "technical institution" under Section 2(h) of the AICTE Act expressly excludes a "University". Consequently, statutory provisions designed for technical institutions cannot be automatically extended to Universities.
  • Scope of Section 10(1)(k): The power conferred on AICTE under Section 10(1)(k) to grant approval specifically confines the limits of such power vis-à-vis technical institutions, and does not mandate prior approval for Universities to establish departments or launch new degree courses.
  • Regulations Ruled Ultra Vires: Regulations framed by AICTE under Section 23 of the AICTE Act (including Regulation 4), to the extent that they compelled Universities to seek and obtain prior approval, were declared null, void, and unenforceable.
  • Duty to Conform to Quality Standards: The Apex Court recorded that although a University does not require prior approval to establish a department or course in technical education, it holds a duty to conform to the standards and norms laid down by AICTE for ensuring coordinated development and quality maintenance.
Official Clarification

3. Official Clarification from AICTE under RTI Act, 2005

The All India Council for Technical Education (A Statutory Body of the Government of India) has formally reaffirmed this legal position in official correspondence under the Right to Information Act, 2005:

Reference Number Issuing Authority Date Official Clarification
F. No. 14-270/RTI/PIO/PC/2014 Assistant Director & PIO (PC), All India Council for Technical Education, Chanderlok Building, Janpath, New Delhi 8th August, 2014 "It is not mandatory for the Universities/Deemed Universities to seek prior approval of AICTE for starting the Technical Programme/Course. However, universities have obligation or duty to conform to the standards and norms laid-down by the AICTE and in terms of the observations of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Bharathidasan case."
Public Notice

4. AICTE Public Notice on Regulatory Framework

In its statutory Public Notice (Advt. No. UB/04(03)/2010), the All India Council for Technical Education clarified the legal scope for technical education across the nation:

  • Definition of Technical Education (Section 2(g)): Encompasses programmes of education, research, and training in engineering, technology, architecture, town planning, management, pharmacy, applied arts and crafts.
  • Definition of Technical Institution (Section 2(h)): Defines a technical institution as an institution not being a University offering courses of technical education.
  • Mandatory Approval for Affiliated Institutions: Affiliated colleges, private polytechnics, and non-university institutions offering technical programmes or franchised arrangements are required to obtain prior AICTE approval.
  • Universities Governed by Apex Court Verdict: Autonomous Universities are governed by the Supreme Court holding in the Bharathidasan University case, operating under their respective statutory Acts while maintaining compliance with AICTE quality standards.
Quality Assurance

5. Academic Quality & Standards at Dev Sanskriti Vishwavidyalaya

Dev Sanskriti Vishwavidyalaya strictly follows the academic standards, curriculum frameworks, and infrastructural recommendations formulated by AICTE across all technical, technological, and management programs:

  • AICTE Model Curriculum: Syllabi and course structures for technical and vocational offerings are updated in line with AICTE model curricula and NEP 2020 guidelines.
  • Faculty Qualifications: Faculty members meet or exceed the eligibility criteria, qualifications, and cadre ratios prescribed by UGC and AICTE.
  • Modern Laboratory Infrastructure: Well-equipped computer laboratories, engineering workshops, software suites, high-speed campus networking, and digital learning resources.
  • Degree Recognition: All undergraduate, postgraduate, and diploma qualifications awarded by Dev Sanskriti Vishwavidyalaya are legally recognized throughout India for Central and State Government employment, public sector undertakings, and higher studies worldwide.
